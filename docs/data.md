# The league dataset

Everything in `static/data/` is generated by the pipeline and never hand-edited:

```
python3 scripts/pull-league-history.py   # Sleeper -> league-history, weeks, transactions,
                                         #   ownership, keepers, players
npm run derive-site-data                 # -> games.json, season-notes.json
npm run derive-narratives                # -> narratives.json (and docs/league-lore.md)
```

The pull walks `previous_league_id` back from `sleeperLeagueId` and pulls every season. The files
live in `static/` so they are served as-is and fetched on demand rather than bundled into the JS.

| file | what it is | fetched by pages? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `league-history.json` | seasons, managers, records, drafts, brackets, final standings, traded picks, `nfl_state` | yes |
| `games.json` | one row per team per game, columnar and small — **the history pages' main source** | yes |
| `season-notes.json` | per-season trades, top scorers, high and low weeks | yes |
| `keepers.json` | keeper chains, cost in rounds, rule check | yes |
| `players.json` | player_id → name / position / team | yes |
| `weeks.json` | weekly roster snapshots — the time machine | no |
| `transactions.json` | every add / drop / waiver / trade | no |
| `ownership.json` | player → who rostered them, when | no |
| `narratives.json` | generated facts for the lore and the blog | no |

The big files (`weeks.json`, `transactions.json`) are never fetched by a page. Anything new that
needs them gets a derived, size-budgeted file, the way `games.json` exists for `weeks.json` and
`season-notes.json` for `transactions.json`.

Manager IDs everywhere are Sleeper `user_id`s, matching the keys of `managers` in league.json.
Player IDs resolve through `players.json`.

## `games.json` — the game table

Every completed game, one row per team (so each game appears twice), stored columnar:

```
generated, count
through      {season, week}  the last completed week -- the "data through week N" stamp
nfl_state    copied from league-history.json, the input that decided "completed"
managers[]   user_ids; the manager and opponent columns are indexes into this
kinds[]      ["regular", "playoff", "placement", "consolation", "exhibition"]; kind indexes it
columns[]    ["season", "week", "manager", "opponent", "pf", "pa", "result", "kind", "max_pf",
              plus "median" and "leg" only in leagues that have them]
data[][]     one array per column, in that order, all the same length
```

`season` and `week` are numbers; `pf`/`pa` are the official score to the hundredth (a
commissioner's `custom_points` wins over `points`); `result` is `"W"`, `"L"` or `"T"`. Rows are
sorted by season, week, user_id. In the browser, `getLeagueGames()` in
`src/lib/utils/helperFunctions/leagueGames.js` decodes it to row objects.

- **`median`** (median leagues only): the week's result against the league median, `"W"`, `"L"`,
  `"T"` or null. Sleeper's records count it; head-to-head never does.
- **`leg`** (two-week playoff rounds only): 1 or 2 for the two weeks of one round.

What is in and out:

- **Completed weeks only**, judged per week from `nfl_state` (not per season): a half-played
  week has real points in it and still isn't a result.
- **No byes and no empty week 18.** Rows with a falsy `matchup_id` are not games.
- **`kind` comes from the brackets, never the week number.** Bracket rows carry roster IDs,
  mapped through that season's own roster map. Winners-bracket games are `playoff`, except the
  placement games below the title (`p` of 3 or more), which are `placement`; every losers-bracket
  game is `consolation`. Weeks before `playoff_week_start` are `regular`. A playoff-week game
  between two teams in neither bracket is `exhibition`: a real result that decides nothing.

The script refuses to write anything (exit 1) unless every row has its mirror, every week's best
lineup is at least what was scored, and the brackets are complete. Each manager-season's W/L/T
and points are compared with Sleeper's `records`, and its best lineups, built the way Sleeper
builds them, with Sleeper's `potential_points`. A disagreement there is a warning that prints the
`overrides` entry in league.json that would pin it (`pipeline.strict` makes it a failure); stat
corrections that pair across one game are recognised and reported.

### `max_pf` — the best legal lineup

`max_pf` is the most points the team could have scored that week from everyone on its roster,
filling the league's starting slots (`scripts/lineup.py` knows every Sleeper slot). The bench is
`max_pf - pf`, and lineup efficiency is `pf / max_pf`; `leagueGames.js` has `benchPoints(row)`,
`lineupEfficiency(row)` and `lineupTotals(rows)`.

- **Override games are `null`.** `max_pf` is computed from player points, so on a game whose
  score a commissioner overrode, `max_pf - pf` would measure the ruling rather than the bench.
  Anything summing `max_pf` must skip nulls, and skip the same rows' `pf` when it forms a
  ratio — `lineupTotals()` does.
- **Positions Sleeper actually allowed.** `players.json` holds a player's current position.
  Eligibility is Sleeper's `fantasy_positions`, plus any position the season's own lineups
  prove, plus `overrides.eligibility` in league.json for the rare player that still misses.
- **The true best lineup, not Sleeper's.** Sleeper's `potential_points` fills slots in order
  and can bench a better player (a TE who is also QB-eligible, say). `max_pf` solves the
  assignment exactly, so it is never below what the team scored, and can exceed Sleeper's
  figure in those weeks.
- **IR is not modelled.** `weeks.json` does not record injured reserve, so an IR player who
  scored is in the pool. Sleeper's `potential_points` has the same pool, which is why the
  check can be exact.

## `season-notes.json`

`seasons.<year>`:

```
through_week   last completed week in that season
trades[]       {week, date, id, teams: [{user_id, players[], picks[], faab}]}
               completed trades only; each team lists what it RECEIVED.
               players: {id, name, pos}; picks: {season, round, original_owner}
               (original_owner is a user_id, resolved through that season's roster map);
               faab: dollars received
top_scorers    {user_id: [{id, name, pos, points, starts}] x3}
               points scored in the STARTING lineup, every completed fixture week,
               playoffs and consolation included -- the same rule as derive-narratives'
               season MVPs, so the site and the blog agree
high_weeks[]   the season's three highest single-team scores
low_weeks[]    ... and three lowest: {week, user_id, opponent_id, points, kind}
```

Pre-draft trades are filed under week 1 of the league they happened in, so a trade can move the
same season's picks.

## `final_standings` and `traded_picks` (in `league-history.json`)

`final_standings.<season>[]` is `{place, user_id, source}`, derived from the playoff brackets
rather than hand-kept. Sleeper marks placement games with `p`: in the winners bracket p=1 is the
title game and p=3 third place; the losers bracket repeats the pattern below the playoff teams
(or, in a toilet bowl, from the bottom up). Teams no placement game reaches are ordered by
regular-season record then points for, and carry `source: "regular season"`.

Only seasons with a completed bracket appear. An unplayed season is omitted rather than ranking
0-0 teams and inventing a champion.

`traded_picks.<draft season>[]` is `{round, original_owner, current_owner, previous_owner,
recorded_on_season}`. Pick ownership is filed against the league that was current when the
trade happened, but `season` says which draft the pick is *for*, so entries appear under future
seasons; the same pick can be listed more than once as it changes hands, so dedupe on
`(round, original_owner)` for a final picture.

## `weeks.json` — time travel

`weeks.<season>.<week>.<user_id>` gives that manager's exact state that week:

```
roster_id, matchup_id, points
starters[]         player_ids in lineup order (the league's roster_positions)
starters_points[]  parallel array of what each starter scored
players[]          the FULL roster that week, starters + bench
players_points{}   player_id -> points, for everyone rostered
custom_points      ONLY where a commissioner overrode the score; then it is the official one
```

Sleeper's records and season points use `custom_points` where it exists and `points` does not,
so anything that totals points must prefer `custom_points` when present. `derive-site-data`,
`derive-narratives` and `week-facts` all do, for results, margins, highs and lows and records;
player-level scoring, and bench and optimal-lineup maths, stay on the computed `points`.

This is the only reliable record of who was on a roster at a given moment — a real snapshot, not
something reconstructed from transactions. Pair two teams by `matchup_id` to get the
head-to-head, and walk weeks 1..N summing wins to build standings as of any week.

**Only completed weeks are written**, and `seasons.<year>.weeks_played` lists exactly the weeks
present. Sleeper serves every scheduled week of the current season as soon as the schedule
exists — real `matchup_id`s, every score 0.0 — and the week in progress part-scored. The pull
drops both: for the season in progress a week counts only once the NFL calendar has moved past
it (`week < nfl_state.week` during the regular season), and only if somebody scored a point in
it. Every week of a completed season is kept, including the empty week 18 (see `has_matchup()`
in the derive scripts).

## `nfl_state` (in `league-history.json`)

`{season, season_type, week, fetched}` — Sleeper's `/state/nfl` at the moment of the pull, with
`fetched` as a UTC timestamp. It is the one input the completed-week rule takes from outside the
league, recorded so that everything downstream of the pull is reproducible from the committed
files alone.

## `ownership.json`

`ownership.<player_id>[]` is a run-length timeline of continuous ownership, derived from the
weekly snapshots: `{u: user_id, s: season, w0: first_week, w1: last_week}`. Use it to answer
"who had him, and when" without replaying the transaction log. Spans stop at the last completed
week.

A new span starts on a change of owner, on a season boundary, **and on any gap in consecutive
weeks**. That direction is deliberate — a gap can only over-split, never wrongly merge two
different owners.

## `keepers.json`

`keepers.<season>[]`, one row per kept player, sorted by pick number:

```
player_id, user_id            who kept him
round / pick_no / cost_round  a keeper occupies the slot it costs, so round IS the cost
previous_season_round         the round he went in the prior season's primary draft
baseline                      "previous season", or "carried-over prior-season draft" when
                              the price comes from an imported pre-Sleeper draft
expected_cost_round           what league.json keepers.rule says it should have cost (null
                              without a rule)
matches_rule                  whether actual == expected
kept_previous_season_by       user_id who KEPT him last season (null if he wasn't kept)
kept_by_same_manager_last_season   drives the repeat penalty
kept_previous_season_too      whether he was also a keeper the year before
held_previous_season_by       user_id who merely rostered him in the last played week
                              of last season -- informational only
```

The repeat penalty keys off `kept_previous_season_by`, not off who happened to own the player at
the end of last season: a keeper traded away mid-season is on the new owner's roster in the
final week, and pricing off that would charge the new owner a penalty they never earned.

## Caveats

- **Roster IDs move between seasons.** Everything is resolved to `user_id`; never join on
  `roster_id` across years.
- **A league imported from another platform has two drafts in its first Sleeper season**, and
  the non-primary one is the old season's draft, re-uploaded. Sleeper files it under the new
  season because that's the league it was attached to; its date is when it was imported. Name
  it in `history.preSleeper[].importedDraftId` and it becomes that season's draft and the
  keeper baseline. Merging the two produces wrong keeper costs. Nothing else of a pre-Sleeper
  season exists in Sleeper.
- **The `keepers` array on a roster object is not reliable history**: Sleeper clears it. It is
  preserved as `managers.<id>.roster_keepers` for the live pre-draft signal only. For history,
  use `keepers.json`.
- **Failed transactions are included** in `transactions.json` on purpose — a losing waiver bid
  records what a manager was willing to pay. Filter `status === "complete"` for moves that
  happened.
- `points_for` on an in-progress season is a running total.
- `players.json` team is the player's *current* team, not their team at the time.
