# League history dataset

Generated by `scripts/pull-league-history.py`, which walks `previous_league_id` back from
the current Sleeper league and pulls every season. Re-run after a season ends and commit
the diff. Nothing here is hand-edited.

These live in `static/` so they are served as-is and can be `fetch`ed on demand rather than
bundled into the JS. ~820 KB total, so fetch only the file you need.

| file | size | what it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `league-history.json` | 114 KB | seasons, managers, records, drafts, brackets, final standings, traded picks |
| `transactions.json` | 259 KB | every add / drop / waiver / trade |
| `weeks.json` | 357 KB | **weekly roster snapshots — the time machine** |
| `ownership.json` | 62 KB | player → who rostered them, when |
| `keepers.json` | 22 KB | keeper chains, cost in rounds, rule check |
| `players.json` | 19 KB | player_id → name / position / team |

Manager IDs everywhere are Sleeper `user_id`s, matching `managerID` in
`src/lib/utils/leagueInfo.js`. Player IDs resolve through `players.json`.

## `final_standings` and `traded_picks` (in `league-history.json`)

`final_standings.<season>[]` is `{place, user_id, source}`, 1st through 10th, derived from
the playoff brackets rather than hand-kept. Sleeper marks placement games with `p`: in the
winners bracket p=1 is the title game and p=3 third place; the losers bracket repeats the
pattern for 5th–8th. The two teams in neither bracket finish **9th and 10th — second to
last and last** — ordered by regular-season record then points for; those rows carry
`source: "regular season"` since Sleeper never assigns them a placement.

Only seasons with a completed bracket appear. An unplayed season is omitted entirely
rather than ranking ten 0-0 teams and inventing a champion.

`traded_picks.<draft season>[]` is `{round, original_owner, current_owner, previous_owner,
recorded_on_season}`. Pick ownership is filed against the league that was current when the
trade happened, but `season` says which draft the pick is *for*, so entries appear under
future seasons; the same pick can be listed more than once as it changes hands, so dedupe
on `(round, original_owner)` for a final picture. Every draft 2023–2026 is **balanced** —
each team ends with the pick count it started with, which is constitution 7.3 holding.

## `weeks.json` — time travel

`weeks.<season>.<week>.<user_id>` gives that manager's exact state that week:

```
roster_id, matchup_id, points
starters[]         player_ids in lineup order (QB,RB,RB,WR,WR,TE,FLEX,FLEX)
starters_points[]  parallel array of what each starter scored
players[]          the FULL roster that week, starters + bench
players_points{}   player_id -> points, for everyone rostered
```

This is the only reliable record of who was on a roster at a given moment — it is a real
snapshot, not something reconstructed from transactions. Pair two teams by `matchup_id` to
get the head-to-head, and walk weeks 1..N summing wins to build standings as of any week.
Weeks with no games played are omitted; `seasons.<year>.weeks_played` lists what exists.

## `ownership.json`

`ownership.<player_id>[]` is a run-length timeline of continuous ownership, derived from
the weekly snapshots: `{u: user_id, s: season, w0: first_week, w1: last_week}`. Use this to
answer "who had him, and when" without replaying the transaction log.

A new span starts on a change of owner, on a season boundary, **and on any gap in
consecutive weeks** — the merge requires `w1 == week - 1`. Every completed season currently
has weeks 1-18 with no holes, so no span is fragmented today; but if a week were ever
missing from `weeks_played`, one continuous ownership run would appear as two adjacent
spans. That direction is deliberate — a gap can only over-split, never wrongly merge two
different owners together.

## `keepers.json`

`keepers.<season>[]`, one row per kept player, sorted by pick number:

```
player_id, user_id            who kept him
round / pick_no / cost_round  a keeper occupies the slot it costs, so round IS the cost
previous_season_round         the round he went in the prior season's primary draft
baseline                      "previous season", or "carried-over prior-season draft"
                              for 2022, whose keepers price off the imported draft
expected_cost_round           what constitution 4.2-4.4 says it should have cost
matches_rule                  whether actual == expected
kept_previous_season_by       user_id who KEPT him last season (null if he wasn't kept)
kept_by_same_manager_last_season   drives the +1 round inflation
kept_previous_season_too      whether he was also a keeper the year before
held_previous_season_by       user_id who merely rostered him in the last played week
                              of last season -- informational only
```

**71 of 75 keepers across 2022-2025 match the constitution exactly.** The four that don't
are listed in the root `CLAUDE.md`; they look like manual commissioner adjustments rather
than a different rule.

Inflation keys off `kept_previous_season_by`, not off who happened to own the player at the
end of last season. Those two agree for all 75 keepers so far, but they can diverge: a
keeper traded away mid-season is still on the *new* owner's roster in the final week, and
pricing off that would hand the new owner an inflation step he never earned.

## Caveats

- **Roster IDs move between seasons.** Everything is resolved to `user_id`; never join on
  `roster_id` across years.
- **2022 has two drafts on the same league, and one of them isn't a 2022 draft.** The
  14-round draft that started 2022-09-04 and carries 18 keeper flags is the real 2022
  season draft, flagged `primary: true`. The 15-round one dated 2022-06-25 is the
  **previous season's draft**, carried over from the league's earlier home on another
  platform and re-uploaded into Sleeper. Sleeper files it under 2022 because that's the
  league it was attached to; the date is when it was imported, not when it was held.
  It is the baseline 2022's keeper costs were priced from, which is why they reconcile
  against it. Any analysis that merges the two produces wrong keeper costs.
- **That carried-over draft is nearly all that survives of the pre-Sleeper season.** No
  transactions, matchups, standings or rosters exist for it — Sleeper history genuinely
  begins with the 2022 season. Don't go looking for the rest; it isn't there.
- **The `keepers` array on a roster object is not reliable history.** Present for 2022-2024
  and partially for 2026 (declarations for the upcoming draft), but empty for all ten teams
  in 2025 — the field gets cleared. It is preserved as `managers.<id>.roster_keepers` for
  the live pre-draft signal only. For history, use `keepers.json`.
- **Failed transactions are included** in `transactions.json` on purpose — a losing waiver
  bid records what a manager was willing to pay. Filter `status === "complete"` for moves
  that actually happened.
- `points_for` on an in-progress season is a running total.
- `players.json` team is the player's *current* team, not their team at the time.
